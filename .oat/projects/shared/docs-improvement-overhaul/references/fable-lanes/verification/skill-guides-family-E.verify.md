## Weakest three sections (junior-to-mid reader, closeout page alone)

Could they finish a project in the right order? **No, not reliably.** The page orders skills PR → retro → revise → document → summary → complete and gives no default sequence. A reader following it top to bottom would open the final PR before updating documentation or writing the summary, and would not learn that pr-final auto-generates the summary anyway.

1. **oat-project-complete** (closeout:342-382). It is dense with internal terms: "read-only closeout invariant", "`complete` or `not_required`", "durable archive flows", "project-ref publication route", "resumable pointer". It never says what the user will be asked, and it omits the explainer-kit requirement, possible PR creation, and the gate-warning confirmation. A newcomer cannot predict the run.
2. **oat-project-autonomous** (autonomy:197-244). This is the highest-consequence skill and has the thinnest safety statement. It does not mention the pushes, the dispatch-policy prerequisite, or the active-pointer-overrides-goal trap.
3. **oat-wave-execute** (wave-workflows:130-170). Its prerequisites are abstract ("repository baseline must be usable", "permissions for the planned operations"). It does not list the concrete branch, merge, delete and archive operations a reader is authorizing.

The runners-up are oat-project-summary (:326-336), whose rerun paragraph is internal jargon, and the missing ordering guidance at the top of the closeout page.

### High

- **H1: Acquire exclusive recovery ownership before replacing pending allocation metadata** (`packages/cli/src/commands/project/archive/archive-utils.ts:3494`; overwriting write at line 3498, destructive retry cleanup at lines 3095 and 3130).

  `takeOverPendingGeneration` reads a pending allocation, unconditionally overwrites its metadata with a new owner token, then verifies the token it just wrote. The pending allocation can belong to a still-running caller, and that caller can complete between this read and write. The write then changes a completed archive back to pending, making the delayed retry appear authorized to clear it.

  The deterministic probe below uses the real public archive function. Call A pauses before copying under its pending claim. Call B reads that pending metadata and pauses just before the takeover write. A resumes, successfully completes the archive and removes the active source. B resumes, overwrites A's completed metadata, deletes its retained files, then encounters ENOENT copying the removed source. For generation 1, B's error cleanup removes the whole completed archive; for generation 2, only pending metadata remains. Both the source and the newly completed retained state are lost. The predecessor is preserved for generation 2.

  This violates D11 and p04-t23/p04-t26's ownership guarantee. The added tests pause B before its initial exclusive creation, so they cover the vacant-path race but never exercise this pending-resume race. A plain owner-token read-back cannot provide atomic recovery ownership or prove the prior caller stopped.

  **Fix:** Hold an exclusive transaction/recovery lock across identity revalidation, takeover, copy, completion and cleanup. Refuse a pending allocation whose caller still holds ownership; recover an interrupted allocation only after acquiring exclusive recovery authority. Re-evaluate pending/completed identity under that lock and keep clearing and finalization inside the same ownership boundary. Add this deterministic overlap case for both first archives and successor generations, requiring A's completed archive to remain byte-identical and B to reuse it or refuse without writes.
